The honest answer to "is our non-compete enforceable" is now, more than ever, "it depends on where you're asking." Courts in different circuits have reached genuinely different conclusions on how narrowly a restrictive covenant must be drawn to survive review, and the gap between the strictest and most permissive jurisdictions has widened rather than narrowed.

## Why this matters even if you don't litigate often

Most non-competes are never tested in court — their value is in deterring a departing employee from joining a competitor in the first place. But an agreement that would not survive a challenge is worse than no agreement at all, because it creates a false sense of protection and, in some jurisdictions, can expose the employer to fee-shifting if a court finds it was drafted in bad faith or with a scope no reasonable employer could have believed was enforceable.

## What we are advising clients to check

**Geographic and temporal scope.** "Anywhere in the world" and "five years" are treated with real skepticism almost everywhere now. A scope tied to the employee's actual customer contact and a duration of twelve to eighteen months holds up far better under review.

**Consideration at signing.** In several jurisdictions, a non-compete presented after employment has already started, without new consideration beyond continued employment, faces a materially higher bar. If your agreements are signed on day one with the offer letter, you are in a stronger position than if they arrive in a policy update eighteen months later.

**Choice-of-law and choice-of-forum clauses.** These provisions are themselves being scrutinized, not just enforced automatically. A few jurisdictions will decline to apply an out-of-state choice-of-law clause specifically to avoid what they view as forum shopping around their own public policy on competition.

**Blue-pencil exposure.** Know whether the governing jurisdiction will narrow an overbroad clause to something enforceable, or void the whole provision. That single difference should change how conservatively you draft.
